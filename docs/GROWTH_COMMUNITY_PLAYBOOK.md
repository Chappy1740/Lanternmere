# Lanternmere Growth & Community Playbook

**Version:** 1.1  
**Purpose:** A practical operating playbook for growing Lanternmere without turning it into an ad machine.

## North Star

Lanternmere is not trying to manufacture an audience. It is building a place World of Warcraft players want to return to and bring friends into.

> **We are not selling Lanternmere. We are inviting people into it.**

The growth loop is:

**Discover → Understand → Become Curious → Follow the Build → Join the Community → Try Lanternmere → Return → Bring a Friend**

Follower count is useful context, but it is not the primary measure of success.

## Positioning

Primary positioning:

> **Lanternmere — Your World of Warcraft home away from Azeroth.**

Emotional promise:

> **Where weary travelers come to forget the troubles of their day.**

Recurring brand phrase:

> **The lantern is lit.**

Lanternmere should feel warm, useful, welcoming, handcrafted, and community-first. It should never sound like a corporate SaaS launch, an influencer funnel, or a generic gaming news account.

### What Lanternmere Is

Lanternmere is an independently built World of Warcraft hub created by one player for friends, guildmates, and fellow travelers. It brings character, adventure, community, and useful WoW information into one home.

### Ownership and Stewardship

Lanternmere is not community-built. Its design, development, product direction, and stewardship remain with its creator. Community members, friends, guildmates, and Founding Travelers may test the product, report problems, and suggest ideas, but feedback informs the vision rather than owning or directing it.

> **Listen to the community. Do not outsource the vision to the community.**

Backend and administrative access are intentionally restricted. The creator remains the primary owner/developer, with access extended only to one or two specifically trusted people when needed. Testers, Discord members, guildmates, and Founding Travelers do not receive backend access by virtue of community participation.

### What Lanternmere Is Not

- Not an official Blizzard product.
- Not a WoW news outlet.
- Not a paid boosting, carry, gold, account, or trading service.
- Not an influencer personality account.
- Not a generic gaming community.
- Not a finished product until the current release is actually ready.

Public profiles and launch material should state that Lanternmere is an unofficial community project and must not imply Blizzard endorsement.

## Audience

### 1. The Founding Circle

Friends, guildmates, and trusted WoW players.

Their job is not to praise Lanternmere. Their value is using it naturally, breaking it, identifying confusion, and telling us what they would actually return for.

### 2. Guild-Centered Players

Players who regularly coordinate with guildmates or a recurring friend group.

Core message: **one home for the people and WoW information you already care about.**

### 3. Character and Progression Players

Players invested in characters, alts, progression, achievements, logs, rankings, or optimization.

Core message: **see the parts of your WoW journey that matter without turning Lanternmere into another spreadsheet.**

### 4. Community-Minded WoW Players

Players who value belonging, shared adventures, and a friendly home between play sessions.

Core message: **the lodge is the product as much as the tools are.**

### 5. Builders and Curious Developers

A secondary audience that may discover Lanternmere through Eydren or GitHub.

The build story can be shared, but player-facing channels should remain about Lanternmere rather than becoming a coding tutorial feed.

## Channel Roles

Each channel has one primary job. Do not publish identical content everywhere merely because a channel exists.

### Instagram — The Window Into Lanternmere

**Job:** visual discovery and emotional identity.

Use for:
- Polished screenshots.
- Short feature demonstrations.
- Before/after development visuals.
- Atmospheric Lanternmere artwork.
- Carousels explaining a feature or journey.
- Occasional Reels when motion makes the feature easier to understand.
- Major milestones and invitations.

Instagram should answer: **“What does Lanternmere feel like?”**

### X — The Campfire Conversation

**Job:** build-in-public updates and conversations with WoW players.

Use for:
- Short development updates.
- Screenshots and clips.
- Questions that genuinely affect product decisions.
- Replies to relevant WoW discussions where Lanternmere can add value.
- Small wins, lessons, and milestone threads.
- Community feedback and acknowledgments.

X should answer: **“What is happening at Lanternmere today?”**

Do not mass-invite or spam communities. X's own Community guidance favors active participation and relevant invitations rather than mass outreach.

### Discord — The Hearth

**Job:** retention, belonging, feedback, and direct community relationships.

Discord is where someone moves from audience member to community member.

Use for:
- Welcome and onboarding.
- Announcements.
- Alpha/beta access.
- Feedback and bug reports.
- Guild/friend discussion.
- Feature previews.
- Community questions.
- Release notes in plain language.

Keep onboarding simple. Discord recommends prioritizing useful default channels, limiting choices, and using onboarding questions to personalize roles/channels without overwhelming newcomers.

### Reddit — Optional / Future

**Job:** no active role in the current growth plan. Lanternmere does not require a Reddit account or Reddit presence.

If Reddit becomes useful later, use it only when a community's rules allow the relevant post. Participate before asking for attention. Reddit explicitly notes that promotional content is not automatically spam, but individual communities can prohibit it or impose their own ratios/rules.

Best Lanternmere Reddit posts should be useful even if the reader never clicks a Lanternmere link:
- “I built this because our guild kept running into X.”
- A useful UX/dev lesson relevant to WoW players.
- A feature demonstration with an honest request for feedback.
- A problem/solution discussion rather than a launch advertisement.

Never coordinate votes, brigades, or ask Discord members to manipulate Reddit visibility.

## Content Pillars

Every post should fit at least one of these pillars.

### 1. Welcome to the Lodge — 20%

Atmosphere, identity, community, Lanternmere story.

Examples:
- Welcome artwork.
- “The lantern is lit.”
- Why Lanternmere exists.
- Founding Travelers.
- Community moments.

### 2. Show the Product — 35%

Actual Lanternmere screens and useful functionality.

Examples:
- The Hearth.
- Travelers.
- Character pages.
- Quest Board.
- Adventures.
- External progress snapshots.
- Guild/community functionality as it becomes ready.

Rule: **show, do not hype.**

### 3. Build in Public — 20%

Human-scale development updates.

Template:

**What changed → Why it matters to a WoW player → Show it → What we learned/what comes next**

Avoid technical jargon unless the post is intentionally aimed at builders.

### 4. Ask the Travelers — 15%

Questions that produce useful product insight.

Good:
- “When you log in before raid, what are the first three things you check?”
- “Would you rather see mains first or your full warband?”
- “What information do you always end up opening a second site for?”

Weak:
- “What feature do you want?”
- Engagement bait with no product purpose.

Close the loop later: **“You told us X, so we changed Y.”**

### 5. Proof From the Community — 10%

Once real testers exist:
- Tester feedback with permission.
- Bugs found and fixed.
- Features changed because of feedback.
- Community screenshots with private data removed.
- Founding Traveler stories.

Never fabricate testimonials or social proof.

## The Content Test

Before publishing, ask:

1. Does this help, show, teach, invite, or start a worthwhile conversation?
2. Is the feature shown actually available, or clearly labeled planned/beta?
3. Would a WoW player understand the post without knowing Lanternmere history?
4. Does it sound like Lanternmere rather than an advertisement?
5. Is any private account, character, guild, credential, or tester information exposed?
6. Is the image/video readable on a phone?
7. Is there one clear next action, if an action is needed?

If the post fails two or more questions, do not publish it yet.

## Voice

### Use

Warm, plainspoken, lightly thematic language.

Examples:
- “Welcome, traveler.”
- “The lantern is lit.”
- “Pull up a chair.”
- “Here is what changed in the Hearth this week.”
- “We built this because…”
- “Tell us where this gets confusing.”

### Avoid

- “Revolutionary.”
- “Game-changing.”
- “The ultimate WoW platform.”
- “You NEED this.”
- Fake scarcity.
- Manufactured urgency.
- Excessive exclamation points.
- Corporate product jargon.
- Pretending Lanternmere is larger or more mature than it is.

## Visual System for Social

### Visual Priorities

1. Lanternmere's approved logo.
2. Dark blue/black night atmosphere.
3. Warm lantern gold/orange.
4. Lodge, lake, mountains, hearth, and travel motifs.
5. Real product UI when demonstrating functionality.
6. Large, readable text with strong contrast.

### Social Asset Types

Maintain reusable templates for:
- Welcome/brand post.
- Feature spotlight.
- Milestone update.
- “Ask the Travelers” question.
- Alpha/beta invitation.
- Release/update card.
- Quote/tester feedback.
- 9:16 story/reel cover.
- X landscape image.

Do not place text close to crop boundaries. Check every graphic at phone size before publishing.

When AI-generated artwork is used, follow the platform's applicable disclosure tools and do not represent generated art as an in-game screenshot or Blizzard-created artwork.

## Accessibility Standard

Marketing is part of the product experience.

- Add meaningful alt text where platforms support it.
- Caption spoken video.
- Do not communicate meaning through color alone.
- Maintain strong text/background contrast.
- Avoid tiny text baked into images.
- Avoid rapid flashing.
- Write hashtags in readable CamelCase when practical.
- Keep essential information in the post/caption, not only in the image.

## Publishing Rhythm

Consistency matters more than volume.

### Pre-Alpha

Target **2 meaningful public posts per week**, plus genuine replies/conversations.

Suggested rhythm:
- Early week: product/build post.
- Late week: story, question, or visual feature post.

Do not force a post when nothing worth sharing happened.

### Alpha/Beta

Target **2–3 public posts per week** when there is enough real activity.

Add:
- Tester discoveries.
- “You said / we changed.”
- Invitation windows.
- Short demonstrations.

### Launch Window

For the 7–10 days surrounding a public release, increase temporarily:
- Teaser.
- What Lanternmere is.
- Feature demonstrations.
- Launch post.
- Getting started.
- Community response.
- First update/fix.

Then return to the sustainable cadence.

## First 12 Public Posts

1. **Welcome to Lanternmere** — approved welcome artwork and origin story.
2. **What is Lanternmere?** — real app screenshots, simple explanation.
3. **Inside the Hearth** — one feature, one benefit.
4. **Why we are building it** — the friend/guild problem behind the project.
5. **Ask the Travelers #1** — one concrete WoW workflow question.
6. **Meet Travelers** — character/community experience.
7. **Build note** — a meaningful change and why it was made.
8. **Quest Board spotlight** — demonstrate the workflow.
9. **The tools we already use** — explain the philosophy behind integrations without overstating availability.
10. **Private Alpha** — what “break Lanternmere” means.
11. **You said / we changed** — first feedback-driven improvement.
12. **The road ahead** — current state and next destination, with planned work clearly labeled.

## Community Engagement System

Use a **give-first** rule.

Before asking a community, creator, guild, or moderator to look at Lanternmere, establish relevance.

### Daily/near-daily lightweight habit

When actively growing:
- Read current WoW conversations.
- Leave a few genuine, useful replies when Lanternmere has something to add.
- Answer every good-faith comment on Lanternmere posts when practical.
- Record repeated questions as potential content, FAQ, or product improvements.

Do not turn engagement into a quota that produces low-quality replies.

## Creator and Guild Outreach

Do not begin with large creators.

Start with:
- Guild leaders.
- Raid leaders.
- Community organizers.
- Small/mid-size WoW creators whose audience aligns with the feature.
- Players who already discuss the problem Lanternmere solves.

### Outreach Formula

**Specific observation → why Lanternmere may be relevant → small ask → no pressure**

Example:

> Hey — I saw your discussion about keeping guild/character information scattered across several tools. I'm building Lanternmere, an independent WoW project aimed at bringing some of that experience into one home. We're heading toward a small private test. If that problem sounds familiar, I'd value your feedback on one screen/workflow. No promo needed.

The first ask is **feedback**, not exposure.

## Private Alpha Growth

Do not make alpha access a fake scarcity campaign.

Start with a small trusted cohort. Give each tester a reason for being invited and a clear mission.

Suggested identity: **Founding Travelers**.

Founding Travelers are trusted early users, not product owners, developers, or decision-makers. Their role is to reveal bugs, confusion, needs, and opportunities through real use. Product decisions remain with Lanternmere's creator.

Measure:
- Invite accepted.
- Account created.
- First meaningful action.
- Returned within 7 days.
- Feedback submitted.
- Bug/confusion discovered.
- Invited/recommended a friend.

The alpha is successful when it improves Lanternmere, not when it produces flattering posts.

## Referral Loop

Once the app is ready for broader testing:

1. Traveler has a useful experience.
2. Lanternmere gives them a natural reason to bring a guildmate/friend.
3. The invited person lands in a clear onboarding flow.
4. Both gain more value because someone they know is present.

Do not bolt on points, spam invitations, or artificial rewards unless a real product need emerges.

## Paid Advertising

**Default: $0.**

Do not boost Instagram posts simply because the button exists.

Paid media becomes worth testing only after:
- A public destination exists.
- Onboarding works.
- Analytics can distinguish visits, sign-ups, activation, and return usage.
- Organic content has identified messages/creative that already resonate.
- There is enough capacity to support incoming users.
- Relevant Blizzard/API/platform terms have been reviewed for the specific campaign.

If those conditions are met, begin with a small, time-boxed experiment rather than an ongoing budget. Optimize for an activation event, not impressions.

Important: Blizzard's published Developer API terms contain restrictions on using Blizzard Developer APIs/Data for marketing or monetization purposes. Before any paid campaign uses API-derived data, Blizzard assets, or integration-specific claims, review the current legal terms and obtain appropriate advice if necessary.

## Reddit Operating Rules — Future Reference Only

Before posting:
- Read the subreddit rules that day.
- Search for recent similar self-promotion posts.
- If unclear, ask moderators.
- Adapt the post to the community; do not paste the same promotion everywhere.
- Disclose your relationship: **“I'm building this.”**
- Stay and answer questions.

Reddit's current guidance says promotional content is not inherently spam, while communities may impose stricter policies; Reddit also recommends organic participation and moderator outreach for community promotion.

## Discord Readiness

Before opening Discord broadly:

- Enable/verify Community features if appropriate.
- Clear server description and rules.
- A short Start Here experience.
- A welcoming general channel.
- Announcements.
- Feedback/bugs.
- No maze of empty channels.
- Roles only when they provide real value.
- Moderation responsibilities defined.
- New member onboarding tested from a fresh account perspective.

Discord's official guidance emphasizes useful default channels, concise onboarding choices, and a healthy, moderated environment.

## Legal / Brand / Platform Guardrails

This playbook is operational guidance, not legal advice.

Before broad launch or monetization changes, re-check current:
- Blizzard Legal terms.
- Blizzard Developer API Terms.
- Blizzard Video Policy.
- Terms for third-party data providers such as Warcraft Logs, Raider.IO, or Raidbots.
- Instagram, X, and Discord platform rules; Reddit rules only if Lanternmere later establishes a Reddit presence.

Standing rules:
- Clearly identify Lanternmere as unofficial and independent.
- Do not imply Blizzard endorsement.
- Do not sell access to features in a way that conflicts with applicable Blizzard/API terms.
- Do not scrape services where scraping is prohibited.
- Do not use third-party logos/assets as if Lanternmere owns them.
- Obtain permission for community member content before featuring it.
- Remove or anonymize private information from screenshots.
- Keep a record of source/permission for marketing assets when practical.

## Measurement Dashboard

Track a small set of numbers weekly.

### Awareness

- Profile visits.
- Meaningful reach.
- Non-follower discovery.

### Interest

- Saves/bookmarks.
- Shares/reposts.
- Meaningful replies/comments.
- Profile-to-link action once a public destination exists.

### Activation

Define one meaningful in-app action before public launch. Examples could include completing onboarding, adding/claiming a character, or completing another core Lanternmere workflow.

Track:
- Account creation.
- Activation completion.
- Time to activation.
- Where users abandon onboarding.

### Retention

- Returned within 7 days.
- Returned within 30 days when enough history exists.
- Active testers/users.
- Discord members who participate rather than merely join.

### Advocacy

- Friend/guild invitations.
- Organic mentions.
- Unprompted recommendations.
- Useful testimonials with permission.

### Product Learning

- Bugs found.
- UX confusion reports.
- Feature requests by frequency.
- Changes shipped because of feedback.

## Weekly Review

Once per week, answer:

1. What did we publish?
2. What generated meaningful conversation rather than empty reach?
3. What did players ask repeatedly?
4. What did we learn about the product?
5. What content should be repeated, changed, or stopped?
6. What is the one most important story to tell next week?

Do not chase every week-to-week fluctuation.

## 30 / 60 / 90 Day Plan

### Days 1–30 — Establish the Lodge

- Complete Instagram and X identity.
- Publish the first 6–8 meaningful posts.
- Capture a reusable screenshot/video library.
- Prepare Discord onboarding.
- Establish the lightweight weekly metrics sheet.
- Start genuine participation in relevant WoW conversations.
- Do not buy ads.

### Days 31–60 — Invite the First Travelers

Dependent on product readiness:
- Launch controlled Private Alpha.
- Introduce Founding Travelers.
- Collect structured feedback.
- Publish first “You said / we changed” content.
- Test which product stories generate qualified interest.
- Begin small, respectful guild/community outreach.
- Keep Discord intentionally small enough to moderate well.

### Days 61–90 — Build the Campfire

If alpha evidence supports expansion:
- Expand trusted beta cohort.
- Publish short feature demonstrations.
- Develop a public getting-started path.
- Identify a few aligned small/mid-size community partners for feedback.
- Evaluate public launch readiness.
- Continue with $0 paid media unless the paid-ad readiness gate is fully satisfied.

## Decision Rules

When unsure what to do:

**Product vs. marketing?** Product wins.

**More posts vs. better post?** Better post.

**Follower growth vs. useful users?** Useful users.

**Broad audience vs. right WoW audience?** Right audience.

**Hype vs. proof?** Proof.

**Automation vs. personal community interaction?** Personal interaction.

**Public invite vs. not ready?** Wait.

**A feature people request vs. a problem people repeatedly demonstrate?** Investigate the demonstrated problem first.
